Yogananda Project

Research and public-domain methodology

Editorial approach

The archive should distinguish clearly between primary sources, later recollections, internal organizational histories, and modern interpretations. Where accounts conflict, the site should preserve the disagreement rather than flatten it.

Family relationships and guru-disciple relationships should be modeled explicitly so readers can trace both kinship and spiritual lineage without conflating the two.

Verification standards

  • Every person, work, lineage link, and media item should eventually carry one or more source citations.
  • Name variants should be normalized while preserving aliases and spiritual names.
  • Dates should identify whether they are exact, approximate, or inferred.
  • Family ties, guru-disciple ties, and institutional affiliations should be stored as separate relationship types.
  • Rights status should be tracked separately from historical importance.

Public-domain focus

The project is especially concerned with materials that can be lawfully republished or described in detail. Each media item or text record should include jurisdiction-sensitive rights notes and provenance where possible.

Expansion path

After this static phase, the content can be normalized into structured data files or a searchable client-side database while preserving GitHub Pages compatibility.

Priority follow-up datasets include Yogananda's family members, his gurus, and the family and disciple circles connected to each teacher.
